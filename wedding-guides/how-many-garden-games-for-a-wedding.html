How Many Garden Games Do You Need for a Wedding?

For a wedding of around 60 to 100 guests, I'd start by looking at four or five different garden games. That gives people some choice without filling every bit of space you've got. A smaller wedding may only need two or three, and a big outdoor reception could make good use of seven.

Those numbers aren't rules. The venue, how long guests will be free to play, and which games you choose can matter more than the size of the guest list.

A starting point for different guest counts

For up to 30 guests, we'd consider two or three games. For around 30 to 60, three or four can work well. At 60 to 100, four or five gives more variety. For over 100 guests, five to seven may be worth considering if you have the space.

The reason those numbers aren't exact is that guests won't all be playing at once. Some will be having drinks, chatting, eating or taking photographs. A reception with 80 guests and only an hour to play might need fewer games than one with 50 guests and a relaxed afternoon outside.

Pick different kinds of games

I'd rather have a mix of activities than lots of games that feel similar. Connect 4 and Noughts & Crosses are quick head-to-head games. Jenga gets people watching as well as playing. Cornhole is good for a bit of friendly competition.

Ring Toss and Dominoes can give guests a quieter alternative, while Limbo suits people who fancy something more active. Not every guest wants to do the same thing, so variety matters.

Think about the space you're using

You need room for people to play, wait their turn and move around without blocking walkways. Seven games across a generous lawn can look great. Seven games crammed beside a drinks queue won't be enjoyable for anyone.

I'd ask your venue where games can go, whether the area will stay available through the reception and whether there's a suitable covered option if the weather changes. The best games area is usually somewhere guests can see as they move between drinks and photographs.

Will the games be there for the whole day?

If guests only have a short window to play, a smaller selection may be fine. If the games can stay throughout the afternoon and evening, more people have a chance to take part when they feel like it.

We offer all-day hire as standard, with delivery and collection times agreed around the wedding. That can give guests several opportunities to play without a fixed tournament or timetable.

Our choice for a typical reception

For 60 to 100 guests, I'd probably start with Connect 4, Jenga, Cornhole and Ring Toss. That gives four different experiences. If the space is generous, we'd add Noughts & Crosses or Dominoes. If the lawn is small, I'd cut the list back rather than squeeze the games together.

And if mini golf is part of your plans, you may need fewer separate garden games because guests already have another activity. Think about the whole entertainment setup, not just the number of wooden games.
