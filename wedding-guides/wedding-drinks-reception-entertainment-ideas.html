Wedding Drinks Reception Entertainment: 12 Ideas for Your Guests

The drinks reception is often the part of a wedding when couples disappear for photographs and guests have an hour or two to themselves. I wouldn't try to fill every minute, but I'd give people a couple of things to enjoy between catching up and getting another drink.

By Jordan & Dayle · 3 November 2026 · 3 min read

The best choice depends on your venue, how long the reception lasts and whether guests will be mostly inside or outside. These are twelve ideas we'd consider, including options you can organise without a hire company.

1. Giant garden games

Connect 4, Jenga and Ring Toss are easy for guests to wander over to without joining a scheduled activity. They work best somewhere visible from the drinks area, with enough room for people to play and watch without blocking paths.

I'd choose a small mix rather than a row of games that all work the same way. Our giant garden games page shows examples if you're comparing styles and sizes.

2. Portable mini golf

A small mini golf course gives guests a short challenge and the option of keeping score. It's worth considering if your venue has a suitable lawn, courtyard or indoor space with room around the holes.

Think about how many groups might want a turn, and don't forget to check whether the venue permits putters and balls in the proposed area.

3. A close-up magician

A magician who moves between groups can work well because nobody has to gather in one place. Ask how many guests they can realistically entertain during your reception and whether their approach suits the atmosphere you're after.

4. Acoustic music

A singer or guitarist can make the reception feel lively without asking people to stop chatting. I'd listen to live examples and check the volume, power requirements and venue restrictions before booking.

5. A caricature artist

This gives some guests a keepsake and others something fun to watch. The important question is how many drawings the artist can complete in the available time. At a larger wedding, not everybody will get one.

6. A simple photo station

A nice spot for photographs doesn't have to mean a full booth. A backdrop, camera and a little space for groups can be enough. Think about who will take the photographs and whether anyone needs to look after the equipment.

7. An audio or written guest book

Guests can leave a quick message while they're waiting for dinner. I'd put it somewhere people will pass rather than in an empty side room, and make any instructions short enough to read at a glance.

8. A quiz about the two of you

Leave a few questions on tables or ask guests to guess things about you. Keep it light and don't make every answer an inside joke that only your oldest friends will know.

9. Croquet, lawn bowls or pétanque

Traditional lawn games can suit a relaxed outdoor reception. Check that the venue allows them and that the lawn is suitable. They may need more room than they first appear to.

10. A food or drink experience

A tasting station or live food demonstration can be part of the entertainment. I'd organise this with the venue or caterer, because staffing, hygiene rules and service times matter more than the presentation.

11. Something for younger guests

A little table with colouring, puzzles or simple activities can help if lots of children are coming. Position it where parents can supervise, and remember it isn't a substitute for childcare.

12. Nothing more than a few good choices

Sometimes the right answer is background music, somewhere comfortable to sit and one or two activities people can try if they fancy it. Guests might be perfectly happy talking to people they haven't seen in years.

How we'd put it together

For a short drinks reception, I'd choose one main activity and perhaps a guest-book station. For a longer gap before the wedding breakfast, adding a second option can be useful. Check the wet-weather plan and setup time before paying deposits.

Most importantly, the reception should still feel like a chance to relax. The entertainment is there to help guests enjoy themselves, not to stop them doing what they came for.
